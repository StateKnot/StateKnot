The experimental opt-in `A2aServerHttpOptions::with_subscription_authorization_rechecks()` is disabled by default. The original `prepare`, unary, stream and bounded-stream implementations remain byte-identical. Other operations, streaming capability and configured numerical limits retain their contracts.

The final workspace actually ran **8 private tests plus 1 real TCP test family**. That family exercises the [17 frozen matrix IDs](evidence/experimental-a2a-subscription-policy-2026-10-16/contract/acceptance-matrix.json) through both REST and JSON-RPC; this is **not 34 independently named libtests**. It covers pre/post denial and unavailability, callback/idle/ready races, all three pending phases under shutdown and disconnect, EOF/error/terminal/event cap, current host-mapping refusal, healthy-task permit reuse while the previous response remains alive, and SendStreamingMessage/GetTask regressions. The direct single-next oracle checks backend Drop and operation-permit release before returning a terminal event/error, independently of SDK eager polling. The TCP fixture is a controlled policy/backend instrument, not an IdP or durable task backend.

The host authorizer is responsible for current policy; the guard does not reauthenticate the bearer token. The initial admission/backend-creation order remains: a policy change during queued admission can create a backend that is subsequently retired before delivery. Submitted headers or buffered bytes cannot be recalled, and a refusal after headers is an existing safe SSE error/termination rather than a promised HTTP401. No periodic recheck or idle task runs while the adapter is unpolled. Noncooperative callbacks are not preempted; actual-clock/shutdown guards refuse after they return. No zero-gap delivery or wall-clock revocation SLO is claimed.
